Two questions decide every landing

Will it have power?

Solar panels need the Sun above the skyline. When it sets, the lunar night lasts up to two weeks, and many landers never wake up.

Can it talk to Earth?

If Earth is above the skyline, the lander can radio home directly. If a hill is in the way, every message needs a relay satellite.

89% and 0%.

The ridge beside Shackleton crater is in sunlight 89% of the year. The crater floor, 20 km away, has not seen the Sun in billions of years, which is exactly why water ice can survive there.

At the pole, moving a few kilometres or slipping the launch a few days can turn a great site into an impossible one. The tools that work this out today are built for specialists. This one is built for everyone.

Why "Rimlight"?

At the pole, almost everything sits in shadow. Only the raised rims of craters catch the light. Finding that rim light is what this tool does.

In film, a rim light is the light behind a subject that traces it with a bright outline against the dark. That's how the polar terrain looks in the navigator: the Sun skims the horizon and outlines the ridges in gold.
