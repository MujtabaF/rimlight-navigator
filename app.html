    How to use Rimlight Navigator

    What it tells you

    Pick a spot on the Moon and a date. The app works out two things a lander needs: sunlight for solar power, and Earth above the horizon to radio home directly. Hills and crater walls from NASA's terrain maps can block either one.

    How to use it to decide

    1. Pick a site on the left, or type in your own coordinates.
    2. Set your landing date in the UTC box, or click the timeline.
    3. Read the verdict on the right. Below it, Best days to land shows the same 30 days as the timeline: tall green bars are the best days. Click one to jump there.
    4. Click + next to other sites (up to 4) to compare them side by side.
    5. Launch slipped? Press Shift + → to move a day later and watch the numbers change.

    Reading the screen

    • Orange is the Sun, which means power. Blue is Earth, which means a direct radio link.
    • 3D view: the white pin is the landing spot. The white line around the sky is the skyline, where the terrain meets the sky. The orange and blue lines are the paths of the Sun and Earth over the 30 days. Where they are dim, the terrain hides them.
    • Skyline strip (bottom): the whole 360° horizon laid out flat. The grey shape is the terrain. Dots show the Sun and Earth paths: bright means in view, faint means blocked. The circles are where they are right now.
    • Timeline (very bottom): 30 days for each site. The top bar is orange when the Sun is up (brighter means more of the Sun is showing). The bottom bar is blue when Earth is up. The white line is now. Where both bars are filled, you have power and a link at the same time.
    • Right panel: how high the Sun and Earth are right now (in degrees above the flat horizon), the countdown to the next sunset or Earthset, solar power, and a 30-day summary.
